# Physics-consistent inverse design of multiscale elastic metamaterial systems

The inverse design of elastic metamaterials for wave control is intrinsically non-unique. A desired macroscopic elastodynamic response can often be produced by multiple, sometimes very different, microscopic geometries. This non-uniqueness becomes especially pronounced in transformation-based elastic cloaking, where the target material distribution is commonly expressed as a spatially varying effective stiffness tensor field C_eff(x,y), sometimes accompanied by an effective density field ρ_eff, C(x,y). 

A common computational strategy is therefore to separate the problem into stages. In our case, first, a continuous neural representation, such as an implicit neural field, is optimized to predict a homogenized material field C_θ(x,y) that minimizes a wave-based objective evaluated through an elastodynamic solver. This stage is attractive because neural fields provide compact, differentiable, and spatially continuous parameterizations of large heterogeneous domains. However, the resulting field is not itself a real microstructure. It is a target effective medium, and each local tensor value must subsequently be realized by a manufacturable unit cell through an inverse homogenization or reconstruction step.

This reconstruction step introduces a second source of error. Even if the first-stage neural field perfectly minimizes the macroscopic wave objective, a finite microstructure dataset and an imperfect generative model cannot guarantee exact realization of every target tensor. The generated geometry may therefore deviate from the prescribed homogenized tensor field, and these local errors can accumulate into a global degradation of the cloaking performance. A natural remedy is to perform a third-stage post-optimization directly on the reconstructed geometry, for example by modifying pixel-, voxel-, or level-set-level variables to reduce the original finite-element loss. While such post-optimization can recover performance, it scales poorly for large three-dimensional systems because it reintroduces high-dimensional geometry optimization and repeated global wave solves.

In this work, we propose to collapse the reconstruction and correction stages into a single physics-guided generative process. Instead of first generating unit cells from a fixed target tensor field and then optimizing the reconstructed geometry, we train a conditional diffusion model to generate realizable microstructures conditioned on effective elastic parameters. At inference time, the conditioning field is provided by a neural field that predicts the spatial distribution of homogenized stiffness parameters. Crucially, the neural field is not treated as a frozen post-processing input. Its final layers are allowed to adapt during diffusion sampling using gradients from the macroscopic finite-element objective. In this way, the model does not merely attempt to reconstruct a previously obtained tensor field; it searches for a nearby, physically realizable member of the non-unique solution set whose generated microgeometry directly improves the target elastodynamic response.

The proposed formulation reframes post-processing for inverse design as a coupled optimization over a continuous homogenized field and a learned realizability prior. The neural field provides a scalable spatial parameterization of the macroscopic material distribution, while the diffusion model restricts the search to manufacturable or dataset-supported unit-cell geometries. Solver-based guidance during generation allows the system to exploit the non-uniqueness of the inverse problem: rather than forcing an exact reconstruction of one arbitrary homogenized solution, the method can move toward another admissible solution that remains effective at the wave level and is more easily realized by microstructure. This offers a potential route toward scalable, physics-consistent inverse design of large elastic metamaterial systems.

## Technical realization

### Existing building blocks

The pipeline is assembled from two subsystems that already exist in the repo, plus a new coupling layer:

- **Conditional diffusion prior over unit cells** — [microstructure_generation_2d/](../../microstructure_generation_2d/). `OccupancyDiffusion` in [model.py](../../microstructure_generation_2d/network/model.py) is a v-prediction / DDIM-style diffusion model over 64×64 binary occupancy (cropped to 50×50), conditioned on a 4-D vector `(C11, C12, C66, vol)` via classifier-free guidance. Training is wrapped by the `DiffusionModel` Lightning module in [model_trainer.py](../../microstructure_generation_2d/network/model_trainer.py) on the `output/ca_bulk_squared/stiffness.h5` dataset of D4-symmetric cells; the per-channel `scaler_C11/12/66` joblib scalers in [microstructure_generation_2d/](../../microstructure_generation_2d/) standardize the conditioning. The unconditional pass uses `CFG_SENTINEL = -1`. Sampling and FEM round-trip of generated cells already work end-to-end in [generate.py](../../microstructure_generation_2d/generate.py) via `sample_with_tensor` and the periodic homogenization in [dataset/cellular_chiral/bulk_stiffness.py](../../dataset/cellular_chiral/bulk_stiffness.py) (`_compute_stiffness` → `_d4_params_from_C`).
- **Neural field for the macroscopic tensor distribution** — [rayleigh_cloak/](../../rayleigh_cloak/). `NeuralReparam` in [neural_reparam.py](../../rayleigh_cloak/neural_reparam.py) is an MLP with Fourier features that maps each cell-center coordinate to a relative correction of `(C_flat, rho)` over the cloak region (see `NeuralReparam.decode` at [neural_reparam.py:181](../../rayleigh_cloak/neural_reparam.py#L181)). It is currently optimized end-to-end against the elastodynamic boundary loss through a `jax_fem` differentiable forward (`ad_wrapper`) by `run_optimization_neural` and the multifrequency / minimax variants. The cloak geometry, cell-center coordinates and `cloak_mask` come from `CellDecomposition` in [cells.py](../../rayleigh_cloak/cells.py), and the FEM driver that builds `fwd_pred` lives in [solver.py](../../rayleigh_cloak/solver.py) (`solve_optimization_neural`).

The new piece is the **physics-guided coupling** between them: during sampling, the diffusion model's conditioning per cell is supplied by the neural field, and in a final tail of denoising steps the field's parameters are updated by gradients from the macroscopic FE solver.

### Algorithm

 1. **Pretrain the diffusion prior (frozen at inference).** Train `OccupancyDiffusion` on the unit-cell database as already done in [model_trainer.py](../../microstructure_generation_2d/network/model_trainer.py). After this stage its weights are never updated again; the diffusion model is used only as a fixed score / denoiser network and a realizability constraint.
 2. **Initialize the neural field on the cloak grid.** Build `CellDecomposition` for the target problem and create a `NeuralReparam` via `make_neural_reparam` ([neural_reparam.py:204](../../rayleigh_cloak/neural_reparam.py#L204)) with output dimension matching the diffusion conditioning, i.e. 4 channels `(C11, C12, C66, vol)` instead of the current `(C_flat, rho)`. Initialize the MLP either (a) by warm-starting it from the analytic Nassar target tensor field by an L2 fit at cell centers, or (b) by running a short pure-FE optimization with `run_optimization_neural` (purely physics-driven, no diffusion prior yet) to obtain a baseline tensor field `C_θ(x,y)`.
 3. **Set up the joint sampler.** Bring up `M = n_cloak_cells` parallel diffusion trajectories — one per cell center `xᵢ` in the cloak — that share the single neural-field instance `Φ_θ`. At each denoising step `t` the conditioning vector for trajectory `i` is `c_i = Φ_θ(xᵢ)`, evaluated through the same MLP / Fourier-feature stack as in [neural_reparam.py:54](../../rayleigh_cloak/neural_reparam.py#L54). The full diffusion state is the batch `x_t ∈ ℝ^{M×1×64×64}`.
 4. **Two-phase denoising loop.** Replace `_sample_loop` ([model.py:111](../../microstructure_generation_2d/network/model.py#L111)) with a version that takes a callable `cond_fn(t) -> Tensor[M, 4]` instead of a fixed `tensor_cond` and exposes the per-step `x_start`:
    - **Phase A (`t ∈ [1.0, 0.3]`, prior-only).** `θ` is frozen. Each step runs the standard CFG update `x_start = x_0 + w·(x_c − x_0)` with `x_c = denoise_fn(x_t, t, Φ_θ(X))`. This is exactly the current `sample_with_tensor` flow with `tensor_cond` replaced by the neural-field output and `θ` detached. The job of this phase is to commit the diffusion trajectories to a plausible neighbourhood of the dataset manifold consistent with the current `C_θ(x,y)`.
    - **Phase B (`t ∈ [0.3, 0.0]`, physics-guided).** `θ` is unfrozen. At each step:
        1. Run the conditioned denoising step with `c = Φ_θ(X)`, obtaining the predicted clean batch `x̂₀(θ)` (still differentiable w.r.t. `θ` through the diffusion U-Net).
        2. Map `x̂₀` to a per-cell tensor estimate. Since rerunning periodic homogenization on every step is too expensive, we instead use a differentiable surrogate `g_φ: occupancy → (C11, C12, C66, vol)`. A natural choice is the regressor already trainable from the same HDF5 (`output/ca_bulk_squared/stiffness.h5`) — see [surrogate/](../../surrogate/). Concretely the per-cell realized tensor is `Ĉᵢ = g_φ(x̂₀,ᵢ)`.
        3. Assemble a global heterogeneous material field from `{Ĉᵢ}` at the cell centers (using `CellDecomposition.expand_to_quadpoints`, [cells.py](../../rayleigh_cloak/cells.py)), call the existing `fwd_pred` built in [solver.py:231](../../rayleigh_cloak/solver.py#L231) (`ad_wrapper(problem, ...)`), and evaluate the macroscopic cloaking loss `L_cloak(u, u_ref)` from [optimize.py](../../rayleigh_cloak/optimize.py).
        4. Backpropagate `L_cloak` through the FE adjoint → surrogate `g_φ` → `x̂₀` → diffusion U-Net → `Φ_θ` and take an Adam step on `θ` only (the diffusion weights and `g_φ` stay frozen). The diffusion update of `x_t` itself stays inside the no-grad path; only `θ`'s gradient leaves Phase B.
 5. **Final realization and verification.** After the last denoising step, the binary cells `x_0,ᵢ` are cropped 64→50 ([generate.py:33](../../microstructure_generation_2d/generate.py#L33)) and (a) each is FE-homogenized with `_compute_stiffness` to get the true realised tensors, (b) the full assembled cloak is run through `solve` ([solver.py:85](../../rayleigh_cloak/solver.py#L85)) once to report the actual elastodynamic objective achieved. These are the numbers compared against the baselines listed above.

### Cross-framework bridge (PyTorch ↔ JAX)

The diffusion stack is PyTorch and the FE / neural-field stack is JAX. This bridge is already implemented in [latent_ae/fem_bridge.py](../../latent_ae/fem_bridge.py) and is reused as-is:

- `build_freq_targets(base_config, f_stars)` ([fem_bridge.py:65](../../latent_ae/fem_bridge.py#L65)) mirrors the setup in `rayleigh_cloak.solver.solve_optimization_neural` — it builds the full / cloak meshes, `CellDecomposition`, per-frequency reference solves, FE problems, `ad_wrapper`-wrapped forwards and the loss targets — and returns a `FEMContext` holding one `jax.value_and_grad` closure per frequency plus a `ThreadPoolExecutor` for running them concurrently.
- `MultiFreqFEMLoss` ([fem_bridge.py:170](../../latent_ae/fem_bridge.py#L170)) is a `torch.autograd.Function` whose forward consumes a single design `(C_grid: (X, Y, P), rho_grid: (X, Y))` of PyTorch tensors, dispatches the per-frequency JAX value-and-grad calls through the pool, blends the decoded cloak-cell values with `params_init` outside the cloak via the `cloak_mask` ([fem_bridge.py:157](../../latent_ae/fem_bridge.py#L157)), and returns a `(n_freq,)` torch tensor of per-frequency losses. The backward scales the saved JAX gradients by `grad_output` and reshapes them back to grid layout — so the FE adjoint plugs straight into PyTorch autograd.

For Phase B we therefore only need to (i) call the surrogate `g_φ` per generated cell to produce `(C_grid, rho_grid)`, (ii) call `MultiFreqFEMLoss.apply(C_grid, rho_grid, fem_ctx)`, and (iii) sum / weight the per-frequency losses (or take the max for a minimax variant) and `.backward()` — gradients flow through the FE adjoint → surrogate → diffusion U-Net → neural field `Φ_θ` automatically. One implementation note carried over from the bridge: `MultiFreqFEMLoss.forward` currently asserts a single design (`C_grid.ndim == 3`), so per-step batching across the `M` cloak cells happens *inside* the decoder and surrogate (which produce the `(X, Y, P)` grid), not by stacking multiple `M`-batches at the bridge.

### Concrete deliverables in the codebase

- New module, e.g. `field_guided_diffusion/guided_sampler.py`, containing:
    - a `NeuralFieldConditioner` (PyTorch port of `NeuralReparam`, outputting the scaled 4-D conditioning expected by `OccupancyDiffusion`);
    - `guided_sample(diffusion, conditioner, cell_centers, surrogate, fwd_pred, ...)` implementing the two-phase loop above (refactor of `_sample_loop`);
    - an Adam optimizer over `conditioner.parameters()` only.
- A small refactor of `OccupancyDiffusion._sample_loop` to accept a `cond_fn` and to optionally return `x_start` with gradients enabled in Phase B.
- New entry script `run_guided.py` (mirroring [run_optimize.py](../../run_optimize.py)) that wires together: loaded diffusion checkpoint → surrogate checkpoint → `FEMContext` from `latent_ae.fem_bridge.build_freq_targets` → `guided_sample`. `CellDecomposition`, cell centers and `cloak_mask` come from inside `build_freq_targets`, so the script only needs to pass the `SimulationConfig` and a list of `f_stars`.
- A new config under [configs/field_guided_diffusion/](../../configs/field_guided_diffusion/) capturing all hyperparameters (phase split, `tensor_w`, learning rate for `θ`, surrogate path, etc.).
- Evaluation harness reusing `_fem_roundtrip` ([generate.py:59](../../microstructure_generation_2d/generate.py#L59)) for the per-cell error and `solve` ([solver.py:85](../../rayleigh_cloak/solver.py#L85)) for the global cloaking metric, against the three baselines from the previous section.
